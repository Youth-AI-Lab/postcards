## Fiche 01 · 12 June · accent pink

**Title:** AI Detectives · how machines actually learn.

**What we ran:**
The week opened on a brainstorm about where AI already sits in daily life, then each team sorted scenario cards, voice assistants, automatic doors, navigation apps, spam filters, calculators, traffic lights, into three piles: definitely AI, sometimes AI, definitely not AI, and wrote its own working definition. The long middle activity put two ways of classifying against each other: first sorting shape cards by explicit rules handed over by the facilitators, then sorting a second set where the criterion stayed hidden and had to be inferred from examples. Test cards and deliberately ambiguous ones showed what a forced decision looks like. The session closed on Teachable Machine, each team training a live vision model on concave and convex shapes, testing it under different lighting, distance and orientation, then filling a synthesis sheet: one idea, one piece of evidence, one question.

**Activity:**
- AI Detectives, How Do Machines Learn · 3h
- Ideation sheet: `YouthAiLab_Ideation_Sheet_UAB_AI_Detectives.pdf`

**Who:**
- 15 teams
- one laptop each

**Mood:**
Sceptical, then hooked. Sorting the cards turned into an argument about the automatic doors, and nobody wanted to give up their pile.

**Skill tree:** AI literacy · supervised learning · training data · forced decision

**Best practice:**
Letting the youth train a model themselves in the last half hour, right after they have argued about what counts as AI, is what moves the machine from magical to explainable. They stop describing AI as infallible and start describing it as pattern recognition learnt from data they chose.

## Fiche 02 · 15 June · accent blue

**Title:** Reactivating the machine · breaking your own model.

**What we ran:**
The teams trained a binary image classifier, then spent the hour trying to break it: changing the lighting, the angle, the distance, the background, adding accessories the model had never seen, and logging every failure in order to deduce the implicit rules it had learnt. A short discussion moved from technical failure to social consequence, with two real cases, blink detection in cameras and automated CV screening, to separate a bias that is a bug from a bias that lands on people. The longest activity was unplugged: a perceptron built from plastic cups, water and measuring beakers, where input weights, the activation threshold and the hidden layers become something you can pour. The session ended on a brainstorm of social uses tied to the Sustainable Development Goals, each idea written with the problem it targets, the pattern the AI would have to recognise, and the risk of misuse.

**Activity:**
- From Data to the Machine's Brain · 3h
- Ideation sheet: `YouthAiLab_Ideation_Sheet_UAB_MachineBrain.pdf`

**Who:**
- 15 teams
- laptops and webcams

**Mood:**
Competitive and slightly gleeful. Finding the exact accessory that fooled the model became the game of the morning.

**Skill tree:** model testing · edge cases · algorithmic bias · perceptron

**Best practice:**
Asking the youth to break the model they have just trained works far better than showing them a broken one. Once they have found the accessory that fools it, the question of who a real system fails lands on its own, without any lecturing.

**Pictures:** `step-02.jpg` · `step-02-b.jpg` · `step-02-c.jpg`

## Fiche 03 · 16 June · accent green

**Title:** Designing an AI for a robot · starting from a need.

**What we ran:**
The session turned from technique to design. Each team picked a Sustainable Development Goal challenge and had to argue that it was actually feasible, not merely desirable. They then filled an empathy map centred on one precise place or user, a classroom, a garden, a recycling area, and condensed what they found into a single problem statement, reframed as a "how might we" question. Ideas were filtered through three criteria: useful, possible, and trainable or programmable. The rest of the morning went into a first layout of the whole chain, input to AI classification to robot action, as a storyboard, a paper mockup, an early training run or a first piece of robot code.

**Activity:**
- Designing an AI with a Robot · 3h
- Ideation sheet: `YouthAiLab_Ideation_Sheet_UAB_RobotAiDesigning.pdf`

**Who:**
- 15 teams
- one robot kit each

**Mood:**
Slower and more argumentative. The feasibility filter killed several favourite ideas, and the teams had to defend what was left.

**Skill tree:** empathy mapping · problem framing · SDG design · system architecture

**Best practice:**
Making the youth argue for the feasibility of their challenge before they design anything is what keeps a week like this honest. It is also the moment the project stops being a technical exercise and becomes theirs, because the need they picked is one they can point at in their own building.

**Pictures:** `step-03.jpg` · `step-03-b.jpg` · `step-03-c.jpg`

## Fiche 04 · 17 June · accent orange

**Title:** From prototyping to testing · and defending it out loud.

**What we ran:**
The last session opened with each team naming what was still missing, splitting the roles and setting a minimum viable product for the day. They then finished the functional core: training or retraining the model, wiring the classification output to a robot action, firming up the physical structure. Cross-testing followed, each team demonstrating to its peers with expected cases, edge cases and cases nobody had planned for, presenters and observers both logging what happened. The week closed on presentations of three to four minutes covering the project title, the Sustainable Development Goal it targets, the user context, the classification logic, the robot action, the tests that passed, the errors found, and what the team would change next.

**Activity:**
- Del prototip a la prova · 3h

**Who:**
- 15 teams
- a model and a robot each

**Mood:**
Deadline energy, then relief. The unexpected test cases handed over by the other teams were the part everyone remembered.

**Skill tree:** prototyping · test logs · error analysis · public presentation

**Best practice:**
Having the youth test each other's prototypes, rather than only their own, surfaces the cases the builders could not imagine. They end the week presenting their errors alongside their successes, and that is the habit worth keeping.
