One week, three causes, and a chatbot that had to prove it.

Five days at the Vent des Îles youth space in La Rochelle, paced by a sealed mission envelope opened every morning. Three teams voted their cause out of a Sustainable Development Goals bingo, then turned a general-purpose AI into a local expert: rules of conduct, personas, a field enquiry around the neighbourhood, a mascot, and a public reveal on the Friday.

01
Monday
Done
SDG bingo · choosing a cause worth a week.
▸
What we ran The week opened with no subject decided. The youth played a bingo built for the workshop on the seventeen Sustainable Development Goals: printed grids on the table, numbers drawn one by one, and each number bringing up a goal and a concrete situation behind it. Thirty situations went through the room that way, argued one after the other, before a show of hands settled which ones stayed. Three causes came out of the vote and became the three teams of the week: spotting wildfire outbreaks from satellite images, mapping coral reefs, and peer mediation against bullying at school.

02
Tuesday
Done
Mission 01 · the heart and the sources.
▸
What we ran Each team gave its chatbot an identity: a name, a mission in one sentence, and the exact territory its cause covers. They then wrote two or three rules of conduct, what it always does and what it never does, plus their own quality requirements: dated figures, named actors, precise places. The afternoon put a general-purpose AI on the spot, with a voice-driven press conference where the whole room questioned it out loud on their subject, rumours and fake news included. The bullying team came out of it with a rule written in their own words: stop inventing sources.

03
Wednesday
Done
Mission 02 · the ears and the voice.
▸
What we ran A day spent on the people who would actually open the chatbot. Each team drew and wrote persona cards: a first name, an age, a job, a family situation, favourite apps, and sliders for how much they know about the cause, how patient they are, how they feel about technology. Then, for each persona, the question they arrive with and the tone the chatbot owes them. The day closed on reference question-and-answer sheets: the question, the answer the team considers right, the sources it must cite, and what it must never say. Seven personas came out of the day, including a conspiracy theorist written by the fire team to trap their own chatbot.

04
Thursday
Done
Mission 03 · the memory, built in the neighbourhood.
▸
What we ran The field enquiry day. Colour-coded cards, one colour per type of information: a figure, an actor, a place or fact, a useful gesture. Every card carries the answer and a line where the source goes. The teams searched online and, above all, went out to question the people around the youth space. The fire team came back with thirty-five filled cards, from the role of the forest office to the four fire-risk sectors of the Charente-Maritime. The bullying team counted how many people out of five knew the 3018 helpline, and what five adults would tell a young person to do. Those cards became the memory of the chatbots.

05
Friday
Done
Mission 04 · personality, red lines and the reveal.
▸
What we ran The last day gave the chatbots a face and a voice. Mascots were drawn and coloured: a smiling flame with smoke for hair for Feurisson, a character carrying a red, blue and yellow shield for Superbot. Each team fixed its tone and its red lines, what the chatbot will never say whatever it is asked, then assembled an identity poster with the QR code that opens it. The fire team kept a constraint it refused to drop: Feurisson answers only in verse, because advice that rhymes is remembered better, and rhyming does not excuse being wrong. The week closed with each team presenting its chatbot to the others and to the adults present, live questions included.
