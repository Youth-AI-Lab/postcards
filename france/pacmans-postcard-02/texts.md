## Fiche 01 · Monday · accent green

**Title:** SDG bingo · choosing a cause worth a week.

**What we ran:**
The week opened with no subject decided. The youth played a bingo built for the workshop on the seventeen Sustainable Development Goals: printed grids on the table, numbers drawn one by one, and each number bringing up a goal and a concrete situation behind it. Thirty situations went through the room that way, argued one after the other, before a show of hands settled which ones stayed. Three causes came out of the vote and became the three teams of the week: spotting wildfire outbreaks from satellite images, mapping coral reefs, and peer mediation against bullying at school.

**Activity:**
- WikiYouthBot, opening the week
- Link: WikiYouthBot ideation sheet

**Who:**
- 11 researchers
- 12 to 15 yo, 3 teams

**Mood:**
Noisy and competitive, the way a bingo should be. Thirty situations, thirty arguments, and no adult deciding the winner.

**Skills:** SDG literacy · framing a problem · collective decision · teamwork

**Best practice:**
A game that puts thirty situations on the table before any vote is what makes the choice real. At twelve to fifteen, the value sat as much in the arguments traded over each situation as in the three causes that came out, and the youth defended them all week because they had picked them.

## Fiche 02 · Tuesday · accent orange

**Title:** Mission 01 · the heart and the sources.

**What we ran:**
Each team gave its chatbot an identity: a name, a mission in one sentence, and the exact territory its cause covers. They then wrote two or three rules of conduct, what it always does and what it never does, plus their own quality requirements: dated figures, named actors, precise places. The afternoon put a general-purpose AI on the spot, with a voice-driven press conference where the whole room questioned it out loud on their subject, rumours and fake news included. The bullying team came out of it with a rule written in their own words: stop inventing sources.

**Activity:**
- WikiYouthBot, mission 01
- Link: the AI press conference

**Who:**
- 11 researchers
- 12 to 15 yo, 3 teams

**Mood:**
Sceptical, then loud. Catching the AI citing a source that did not exist turned the room against it.

**Skills:** system prompt · source criticism · hallucination spotting · writing rules

**Best practice:**
An AI answering out loud, to the whole room, gets caught much faster than the same AI typing on a private screen. At this age the collective reaction is what turns a vague suspicion into a rule the youth are willing to write down and enforce.

## Fiche 03 · Wednesday · accent blue

**Title:** Mission 02 · the ears and the voice.

**What we ran:**
A day spent on the people who would actually open the chatbot. Each team drew and wrote persona cards: a first name, an age, a job, a family situation, favourite apps, and sliders for how much they know about the cause, how patient they are, how they feel about technology. Then, for each persona, the question they arrive with and the tone the chatbot owes them. The day closed on reference question-and-answer sheets: the question, the answer the team considers right, the sources it must cite, and what it must never say. Seven personas came out of the day, including a conspiracy theorist written by the fire team to trap their own chatbot.

**Activity:**
- WikiYouthBot, mission 02
- Link: the personas, on the Feurisson documentation page

**Who:**
- 11 researchers
- 12 to 15 yo, 3 teams

**Mood:**
Absorbed and funny. The personas got backstories, pets and terrible taste in apps before they got a single question.

**Skills:** persona design · audience awareness · tone of voice · reference answers

**Best practice:**
Asking the youth to write a persona who disagrees with their chatbot is what pushes the work past decoration. Building their own opponent forced them to decide, in writing, how the chatbot answers someone it will not convince: never confirm without proof, never be brusque, keep asking questions.

## Fiche 04 · Thursday · accent pink

**Title:** Mission 03 · the memory, built in the neighbourhood.

**What we ran:**
The field enquiry day. Colour-coded cards, one colour per type of information: a figure, an actor, a place or fact, a useful gesture. Every card carries the answer and a line where the source goes. The teams searched online and, above all, went out to question the people around the youth space. The fire team came back with thirty-five filled cards, from the role of the forest office to the four fire-risk sectors of the Charente-Maritime. The bullying team counted how many people out of five knew the 3018 helpline, and what five adults would tell a young person to do. Those cards became the memory of the chatbots.

**Activity:**
- WikiYouthBot, mission 03
- Link: the key data cards

**Who:**
- 11 researchers
- 12 to 15 yo, 3 teams

**Mood:**
Out of the room and back again. Interviewing strangers was the hard part, comparing the answers was the fun part.

**Skills:** field enquiry · dataset building · sourcing · data literacy

**Best practice:**
A source line on every card is what makes the difference. Several cards came back marked "source: AI" in the youth's own handwriting, next to others marked with a ministry website or an interview, and that visible gap is far more convincing than any lecture on verifying information.

## Fiche 05 · Friday · accent yellow

**Title:** Mission 04 · personality, red lines and the reveal.

**What we ran:**
The last day gave the chatbots a face and a voice. Mascots were drawn and coloured: a smiling flame with smoke for hair for Feurisson, a character carrying a red, blue and yellow shield for Superbot. Each team fixed its tone and its red lines, what the chatbot will never say whatever it is asked, then assembled an identity poster with the QR code that opens it. The fire team kept a constraint it refused to drop: Feurisson answers only in verse, because advice that rhymes is remembered better, and rhyming does not excuse being wrong. The week closed with each team presenting its chatbot to the others and to the adults present, live questions included.

**Activity:**
- WikiYouthBot, mission 04
- Link: the productions

**Who:**
- 11 researchers
- 12 to 15 yo, 3 teams

**Mood:**
Last-minute and proud. Colouring the mascot while the poster was still drying, then defending the whole thing out loud.

**Skills:** character design · red lines · AI configuration · public presentation

**Best practice:**
Drawing the mascot is not decoration, it is when the rules become concrete. At twelve to fifteen, giving the chatbot a face is the moment the youth start saying "it would never answer that", and the red lines get written by themselves.

## What the week shipped (visible when open)

### Header

- Title: **What the week shipped**
- Label: `Published as the teams left them`

### Items

**Fire prevention · Feurisson.**
A chatbot that answers only in verse, cites three sources before it speaks, and covers the fire-risk sectors of the Charente-Maritime.

**School bullying · Superbot.**
A chatbot answering from a sourced base, which puts personal distress ahead of every other rule and points to an adult of trust.

**Robot prototype · Robo-Caillou.**
A neighbourhood watch robot born from the enquiry, whose eyes follow the official fire vigilance level and which never sprays a person, only an ember.
